TL;DR

Robot Sim Studio (RSS) is a toolkit for building interactive physics simulations of robot manipulation on Newton, GPU physics on Warp and CUDA. Rigid parts, cables, cloth and foam share one scene, each on a solver named in data, and anything in it can be grabbed and dragged in a browser while it runs. A live page rebuilds a scene file on every save, so a coding agent can turn a photo or a clip of a real bench into a simulated twin, one save at a time.

Case studies

Each demo below is the live simulation, embedded. Press Drive the robot to take the robot's hand; until then the page scrolls past it. Until someone grabs it, each robot runs a preprogrammed motion; the hand turns blue while a person drives. One person drives at a time and everyone else watches the same simulation. On a phone the demo opens full screen.

Case study 1 · Contact-rich wiping

Wipe the bar

A chrome grab bar bolted into two black blocks, a blue marker stripe along its crown, and a kitchen sponge. The right arm presses and wipes while the left arm steadies the bar. The stripe comes off through a removal field fed by the sponge's own contact patch, so only real pressure cleans. The scene is the twin of a real clip, rebuilt by a coding agent.

How to drive

Until you grab it, the robot runs a preprogrammed motion. When you grab the hand and it turns blue, you're driving.

  • ViewDrag empty space to turn; right-drag or two fingers to pan; scroll or pinch to zoom.
  • ButtonsNew stripe re-inks the bar; Reset view brings the camera back.

Case study 2 · Cable routing

Route the cable

A 45 cm, 5 mm cable runs from a socket; lay it into three Y-prong clips. The cable is SuperDex's Cosserat rod, and every rigid shape it can touch is mirrored into SuperDex, so the cable feels the arm, the bench and the clips that Newton simulates. The hand holds the cable's end with node constraints re-aimed every substep.

Case study 3 · Cloth folding

Fold the towel

A terry towel simulated with VBD and self-contact. The robot takes the towel's edge by itself, then hands it to you: lift the edge, carry it over, fold it in half. Only the patch under the fingers is held; the rest of the cloth drapes, slides and bunches on its own. The task scores after SoftGym's ClothFold.

Case study 4 · Adhesive peeling

Peel the film

The protective film on a phone, held down by a field of adhesive bonds after AutoPeel (ICRA 2025). The left hand holds the phone; the right has the film's tab and pulls it along the peel arc. Each bond lets go when it is stretched past its limit, one row at a time.
